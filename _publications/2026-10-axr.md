### Author

Runguo Li (single author).

### Abstract

LLM agents answer analytical questions by exploring databases through SQL, and they are commonly run several times per question for reliability. On large data, the database then takes a large share of the agents' time, and much of its work is redundant: attempts repeat their siblings' statements, often while these are still running, and recompute the expensive parts of their own earlier statements under new statement text.

**AXR** is a PostgreSQL extension that materializes the results of plan subtrees as queries run and substitutes them into later plans. Its resolved signatures bind the run-time parameters of PostgreSQL's parameterized nested loops and sub-query plans to the expressions they carry, so the subtrees that dominate agent queries can be matched. A statement can wait for a result that a concurrent attempt is still building, without risk of deadlock. A result is kept only if recomputing it costs more than reading it back, and reuse stays correct under concurrent writes through the same server.

### Key Findings

- Replaying twelve workloads recorded from one agent with two LLMs, AXR reduces database time by **6.5–51.0%** at TPC-H scale factor 10 and by **23.9%** on a TPC-H workload at scale factor 100.
- Where attempts converge, a statement cache that shares running statements obtains most of this saving (21.6% at scale factor 100); reusing subtrees adds **21.3–23.0 points** on every recording of one held-out set of TPC-DS questions, but only 2.0–8.9 on a second.
- In **776,114** checked statement executions and **1,958** random queries compared value by value, AXR returned no wrong result.
