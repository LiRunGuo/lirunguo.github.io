### Abstract

Feed-forward 4D models reconstruct dynamic scenes by answering point queries, but they are trained on clips of tens of
frames, while real videos run to thousands. Encoding a long video whole exhausts GPU memory, and the official long-video
modes split it into chunks that each predict at their own scale or hand tracks from chunk to chunk, so their accuracy
drops sharply as videos grow. Each mode breaks one of two requirements of point tracking: every track keeps its identity
from the query frame, and all predictions share one scale. We propose MemWin, a training-free online inference scheme that
restores both: every window keeps the query frame and a memory of keyframes spread over the entire past, and is registered
to one scale through the query frame. MemWin runs at constant GPU memory, plugs into four 4D models without retraining,
and improves both official modes on nearly every test set, raising 3D tracking accuracy (APD) by up to 0.64 at 2000
frames. A controlled study, confirmed by predictions fixed in advance on unseen data, also exposes a blind spot of current
benchmarks: offline, one encoding of a few evenly spread frames with interpolated tracks matches dense inference up to
1000 frames at a fraction of its cost, because even ground truth interpolated from 32 frames scores 0.92 APD or higher. We
therefore build HOT3D-Long, real hand–object videos whose short bursts of motion defeat interpolation; there, as on
2000-frame synthetic videos, dense inference such as MemWin pays off. We recommend MemWin for online use, sparse uniform
encoding as the offline baseline, and benchmarks that test the motion interpolation misses.

### Key Findings

- MemWin raises the APD of OpenD4RT's official mode on all eleven test sets and of Point4D's on ten, by up to **0.64**.
- Its GPU memory stays constant: **12.7 GiB** for UniQuery4R at 2000 frames, where whole-clip encoding runs out of memory.
- Offline, uniform sparse encoding matches dense inference up to 1000 frames with **6–39×** faster model inference.
- Ground truth interpolated from 32 frames reaches **0.92–1.00 APD** on current benchmarks; HOT3D-Long's burst point-frames
  show where dense inference pays off.
