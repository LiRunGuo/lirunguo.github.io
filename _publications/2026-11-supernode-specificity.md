### Author

Runguo Li (single author).

### Abstract

Circuit-tracing studies support their claims by suppressing or swapping labelled groups of transcoder features called supernodes, but a successful intervention does not show that the labelled features are what matter. This paper tests those claims against matched random replacements under an activation-matched and an attribution-influence-matched null model, using 2,958 automatically constructed claims and 14 hand-curated claims from two models, two transcoder types, and two task families. Because the selection rule and the null model can change a claim's verdict, the paper gives reporting recommendations and releases the specificity test as a library.

### Key Findings

- Thirteen of the 14 hand-curated intervention claims pass the specificity test.
- With per-layer transcoders, only **0–56%** of claims built with a contrastive selection rule are specific; a held-out causal rule raises this to 44–77% but does not help cross-layer transcoders.
- On two-hop recall, the activation-matched null declares **88–99%** of contrastive per-layer claims specific, while on subject–verb agreement the two nulls agree.
- Most two-hop ablation claims that pass the stricter test beat their null by less than a factor of two.
- Supernode specificity is therefore conditional on both the feature-selection rule and the null model used for validation.
