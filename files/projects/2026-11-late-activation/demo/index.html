Late Activation: interactive demo

RL tuning makes a text-to-image flow model return nearly the same composition for every seed. Late activation lets the base model sample the first, high-noise steps and switches the RL-tuned adapter on below a noise level τ. It needs no retraining and no extra network evaluations.

1. What the switch does, on a 1-d toy

Exact probability-flow paths of a four-mode base distribution. The reward-tilted flow (the role of the RL-tuned adapter) prefers the upper side of every mode and slightly the top mode. Paths follow the base flow above τ (gray) and the tilted flow below it (orange).

2. Samples from the same noise

SD3.5-Medium tuned with DiffusionNFT on PickScore (paper scale, 500 rollouts). Each row uses the same four noise seeds; only the sampling schedule changes.

Held-out prompts from the evaluation set; prompts showing real people or named characters are left out.

3. Reward and diversity as τ moves

Held-out results on the same adapter (256 prompts × 4 seeds, mean of two training seeds), relative to NFT on the whole path. Lower τ hands more of the first steps to the base model.

At matched reward, late activation gives more diversity than weakening the adapter on the whole path (velocity weight, LoRA scale) or stopping training early, and matches AIG-u, which needs two network evaluations per step instead of one.
